## Problem

Retail equity research means assembling scattered data — prices, fundamentals, news — into something a
person can reason about, then letting them ask questions of it. As the sole engineer alongside the
cofounder, I owned all of it: front end, backend, data pipelines, and deployment, from a landing page
to 500+ users.

The technical core is that market data has awkward properties. It comes from vendors with their own
rate limits and outages, it is only partly overlapping between vendors, and users expect it to feel
current while also expecting pages to load immediately. Those two expectations pull in opposite
directions.

## Constraints

- Vendor calls were rate-limited and occasionally unavailable, so they could not sit in the request
  path for a page load.
- Coverage mattered: no single vendor was complete, which is why there were two.
- One engineer. Anything requiring ongoing operational attention was effectively unaffordable.

## Approach

The shape that resolved the freshness-versus-latency tension was to move all vendor traffic off the
request path. Cron-scheduled pipelines ingest prices, fundamentals, and news into MongoDB, with
indexes chosen for the queries the product actually issues rather than for the shape the vendors
return. Requests then read from our own store, at our own latency.

Inside ingestion, the work is IO-bound fan-out across many symbols and two vendors, which is what Go
is good at: bounded goroutine worker pools fetch concurrently, with the pool size — not the symbol
count — setting the pressure on each vendor.

The front end leans on the same insight. Most pages describe data that changes on a schedule, so they
are pre-rendered via ISR/SSG rather than assembled per request, with access/refresh token auth for
the parts that are user-specific.

The AI side is a separate Python service: token-streamed chat so answers appear as they are generated,
and a tool-calling agent with database access so questions in plain English are answered from the same
store the rest of the product reads. Where an answer is better shown than described, the agent emits a
structured chart specification the front end renders inline — the model chooses what to plot, but not
how to draw it.

## Tradeoffs

**Scheduled ingestion trades freshness for reliability and speed.** Reads are fast and vendor outages
stop being user-visible, at the cost of data being as old as the last successful run. That is the right
trade for fundamentals and news, and progressively less right the closer you get to live prices.

**Pre-rendering trades immediacy for load time**, and inherits the same staleness question one layer up.

**Letting a model choose chart specifications rather than draw charts** keeps rendering deterministic
and reviewable, but constrains what can be visualised to whatever the spec supports.

**Two vendors bought coverage and cost reconciliation.** Any field present in both needs a rule for
which one wins.
